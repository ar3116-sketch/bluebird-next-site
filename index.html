What changes inside

Phone → companion board

BLUEBIRD flies a Pixel phone as its mission computer, with a holder, USB hub and cooling blower. NEXT puts a low-profile companion board in the same slot over the pack. The flight controller still flies the aircraft.

bay 70 × 80 × 13 mm

8 cells → 12 cells, split

One 4S2P pack becomes a 4S3P of higher-capacity 21700 cells: 8 in the main bay, 4 in a new nose bay. The nose cells replace the phone's forward mass, so NEXT trims with no ballast.

Deeper centre body

Centre-body thickness goes from 1.15 to 1.35 of the base section to fit the nose cells. Same planform, same wing, fins and motor.

Cameras move

The thermal camera moves forward to the left of the nose cells, and the mapping camera moves outboard beside the main pack. Both sit on printed trays.

thermal x 102 · mapping x 200, y 105 mm

Room for long-range links

Bays for LTE and an Iridium satellite modem, plus the telemetry radio, all in the nose. Every component sits on a tray screwed to inserts, so changing a supplier means printing a new tray.

LTE · Iridium · telemetry

Printed shell → glass shell

NEXT's centre body is planned as a glass-skinned foam sandwich instead of printed panels. Its internal frames are not designed yet, so the NEXT renders show no internal structure.

est. −80 g of skin

How these renders were made

  • BLUEBIRD is the detailed CAD of the rev C aircraft: every printed part, component and wing part, with harness and fasteners left out for clarity.
  • BLUEBIRD NEXT uses its own outer shape (the deeper centre body). Parts NEXT keeps unchanged (wing spars and joiners, V-node, servos, motor module, ESC, flight controller, GPS, pitot, Remote ID, rangefinder) are BLUEBIRD's CAD in place. The 12 cells are real 21700 cylinders at their planned positions. Every other new item is drawn as its bay envelope, which includes the tray and clearance: the space reserved, not the part.
  • The colours mark systems, not materials. The shell is drawn as glowing glass so the inside reads.
