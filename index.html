BLUEBIRD NEXT

Long-range survey UAV[ Concept ] Concept: layout analysed, nothing built or flown

BLUEBIRD NEXT is BLUEBIRD reworked for the next generation: more capability on every flight, in an airframe made to be built in quantity. A new, deeper centre body carries twelve cells instead of eight, an onboard companion computer replaces the phone, and new bays take LTE and satellite links. The shell comes out of molds instead of off a printer. The analysis says 88.8 km at survey speed, against BLUEBIRD's 57.6.

      What changes inside

      Phone → companion board

      BLUEBIRD flies a Pixel phone as its mission computer, with a holder, USB hub and cooling blower. NEXT carries a low-profile companion board on the underside of the hatch, where the phone rode, so it swings away with the hatch and leaves both packs free to lift out. The flight controller still flies the aircraft.

      bay 70 × 80 × 13 mm · on the hatch

      8 cells → 12 cells, split

      One 4S2P pack becomes 12 higher-capacity 21700 cells in two removable packs, 8 in the main bay and 4 in a new nose bay, each on one plug. Charge both in place through a port under the hatch, or swap in a charged pair; ideal diodes keep a mismatched pair from cross-charging. The nose cells replace the phone's forward mass, so NEXT trims with no ballast.

      Deeper centre body

      Centre-body thickness goes from 1.15 to 1.35 of the base section to fit the nose cells: a new body shape with its own molds. The planform, outer wing, fins and motor carry over from BLUEBIRD.

      Cameras move

      The thermal camera moves forward to the left of the nose cells, and the mapping camera moves outboard beside the main pack. Both sit on molded trays.

      thermal x 102 · mapping x 200, y 105 mm

      Room for long-range links

      Bays for LTE and an Iridium satellite modem, plus the telemetry radio, all in the nose. Every component sits on a tray screwed to inserts, so changing a supplier means a new tray, not a new shell.

      LTE · Iridium · telemetry

      Printed shell → glass shell

      NEXT's centre body is planned as a glass-skinned foam sandwich instead of printed panels. The frames and bulkhead in the renders are a concept placed to carry the trays, not a finished structure.

      est. −80 g of skin

      How these renders were made

      • BLUEBIRD is the detailed CAD of the rev C aircraft: every printed part, component and wing part, with harness and fasteners left out for clarity.
      • BLUEBIRD NEXT uses its own outer shape (the deeper centre body). Parts it keeps (wing spars and joiners, V-node, servos, ESC, flight controller, GPS, pitot, Remote ID, rangefinder, wiring) are BLUEBIRD's CAD, moved into the deeper body. The new parts are modelled from datasheet sizes and placed at the planned layout: both packs cell by cell with their nickel strips, the companion board and heatsink, both cameras with their lenses and mounts, the LTE, satellite and telemetry radios with antennas, and the trays, frames, inserts and screws that hold them. Every part is checked to sit inside the shell without touching another. The internal structure is still a concept, not a finished design.
      • The colours mark systems, not materials. The shell is drawn as glowing glass so the inside reads.

      Built to scale

      BLUEBIRD is a testbed: its centre body is printed piece by piece and bonded by hand, which suits research and slows production. NEXT is designed from the start to be built in quantity.

      BLUEBIRD testbed~1 weekper aircraft · about 86 h of printing
      BLUEBIRD NEXT~2 daysper aircraft · molded shell and trays, no printed parts
      Per mold set~20 / monthadd a mold set to add capacity

      Molded, not printed

      The centre body is a glass-skinned foam sandwich vacuum-bagged in female molds. Glass keeps the shell transparent to radio, so the antennas stay inside, and the layup is planned at about 80 g lighter than the printed skins.

      est. 110 g shell vs 190 g printed

      Print the molds, not the aircraft

      We print the molds ourselves, so a new set is quick to make in-house. Output grows by printing another mold set rather than printing more aircraft; a glass layup line suits a few hundred aircraft a year, and molded foam is the step beyond about 1,000 a year.

      ~20 aircraft / month per mold set

      Swap-ready bays

      Every component sits on a molded tray screwed into inserts in frames bonded at layup, with plug-in connectors, and each bay is sized for the larger of two candidate parts. A new supplier, or a US-made part, needs only a new tray mold, printed in-house; the shell molds stay.

      6 parts with a drop-in second source

      Capability as a configuration

      The same airframe flies line-of-sight surveys or carries the long-range kit (LTE, Iridium satellite and ADS-B In) in bays that are already there. The motor firewall is drilled for a second motor, so the propulsion can change without new tooling.

      one airframe, several configurations

      Build times and mold output are team estimates for a design that has not been built. BLUEBIRD's 86 h comes from its design-for-manufacture review.

      Conservation

      Thermal and RGB wildlife surveys over large, remote areas, with detection on the companion board. The LTE and satellite bays are for reporting from beyond line of sight, which needs an FAA waiver that is not held.

      Agriculture

      Mapping of fields and pasture with the downward mapping camera. Processing and video recording run on the companion board in flight.

      Done

      1. Size. 1.8 m kept after a span sweep from 1.8 to 2.34 m.
      2. Shape. Deeper centre body (1.35) lofted and re-analysed: 88.8 km, stall 9.6 m/s.
      3. Layout. Every part placed for balance: CG 313.0 mm, 8.0 % margin, no ballast.
      4. Service. Both packs lift straight out with only the hatch open: 3.0 and 2.5 mm to spare, 0 parts in the way.
      5. Internals. 62 new parts modelled and checked: 0 outside the shell, 0 clashes.

      Next

      1. CAD. The centre body with its bays, trays, inserts and glass-shell joints.
      2. Analysis. Mass, aero and structures re-run at the CAD mass.
      3. Bench. The companion board with both cameras: power, heat and drivers.

      What carries over from BLUEBIRD

      • The 1.8 m wing, airfoils, elevons, fins, motor and folding prop
      • ArduPlane on a separate flight controller, with every failsafe
      • The ground planner, its vetted landing sites and the launch dolly

      BLUEBIRD NEXT is a concept for the next version of BLUEBIRD, an open survey drone for conservation and agriculture.
